What we kept, cut & added

vs. the field

Kept — the simplicity

  • Mirrored stat tables. Offense vs. the arm it faces, advantage down the middle. The clearest idea in the category.
  • One dark sheet per matchup. No feed, no infinite scroll — a document you read and act on.
  • A confidence number per market. But re-based on EV, not a black box.

Cut — the noise

  • The 5-star ratings. Stars read as a tout screaming. Advantage is now a measured edge, not hype.
  • Duplicate per-game columns. Season / vs-hand / L15 / rank is enough; the rest was visual weight.
  • The buried decision. No more hunting the bottom of the sheet for the actual play.

Added — trust & the 5 W’s

  • The bet ticket. Side, price, book, stake, grade — the whole What, up top.
  • Clickable lineups → props. Your ask: a batter opens a model'd prop card.
  • A receipts band. Calibration (pred vs. actual), a stress test on the biggest lever, and the closing-line story — proof the number is earned.

Confidence — one ladder, not four

no edge+2%+2–6% · the 54.7% band+6%+8% — too big, market knows

Today the app shows conviction four different ways — conic dials, speedometer gauges, calm rings, and A–F letter grades, sometimes on one screen. This collapses them into a single ladder tied to EV, centered on the +2–6% band where a 54.7% win rate lives — the line between break-even and pro, and the number the model is named for. The anti-tout twist: a huge edge isn't a lock, it's a VERIFY flag — on efficient markets a +8% model edge usually means we're missing news, not that the book is wrong.

Say this
  • "Model 55%, market implies 51%. Small edge, quarter-Kelly."
  • "Passed the slate — nothing cleared the bar."
  • "We were wrong here; graded and logged."
Never this
  • "Lock of the day." "Can't-miss." "Easy money."
  • "Turn $100 into $10K."
  • Quietly deleting a losing pick.
